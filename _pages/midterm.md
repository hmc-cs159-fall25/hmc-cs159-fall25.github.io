The midterm will take place on the **week of October 27**. It will consist of **two parts**, both of which are **take-home**. The parts are worth 50 points each, for a total of 100 points.

**Please read the following details carefully** to avoid confusion, as the two parts have different formats and policies!

## Multiple-choice and short-answer questions (INDIVIDUAL)

One part of the exam will consist of traditional short-answer and multiple-choice questions covering concepts from the lectures. There will be 10 questions worth 5 points each, for a total of 50 points. This part of the exam will take place entirely on Gradescope; answers will be submitted digitally on the Gradescope interface, just like CS70 proficiency check questions.

The questions will be **released Monday, October 27 at 12 noon**. They are **due by Friday, October 31 at 5:00 pm**. You may take this part of the exam at **any point** in that window, but once you start, you must complete all 10 questions within **a single 1-hour sitting**. Under the HMC Honor Code, you are expected to keep track of the time limit yourself (the Gradescope submission will ask you to report your start and end times).

This part of the exam is **open-book and open-notes**. Specifically, you are allowed to access any of the following resources:
- The course textbook
- Slide decks from the lectures
- Pages on the course website (i.e., any page whose URL starts with `cs.hmc.edu/cs159`)
- Your past lab journals
- Any notes you have taken

All other resources are off-limits for this part of the exam. You **may not** seek help from other textbooks, online resources, or Google/ChatGPT/etc. In particular, while you are allowed to access pages on the course website, **you may not access any external URLs that happen to be linked from the course website**. 

**This part of the exam must be completed individually**; you may not seek help from your peers or anyone else.

Your adherence to these policies is governed by the HMC Honor Code (even if you are an off-campus student).

## Case study (GROUP-BASED)

The other part of the exam will consist of a _case study_. In this part of the exam, you will be given a description of a proposed NLP project (like the kind of thing a VC might hear as a pitch from a startup founder, or more down to earth, the kind of thing the Clinic directors might hear from prospective sponsors). **In a group of 3-4 people**, you will be asked to provide a **1-page written evaluation of the proposal**. Your evaluation should cover the following points:

- What NLP techniques from class might be relevant to this project?
- What clarifying questions would you want to ask to the proposer?
- What kinds of data might be needed to make this project work? (Be specific; e.g., if labeled data is required, explain exactly what labels are needed)
- Based on the above, what is your overall impression of the feasibility of this project?

Your evaluation will be judged along the same 4-category rubric as the journals: _Insight_ for how well your response integrates course concepts, _Correctness_ for whether or not all the claims you make are factually accurate, _Completeness_ for whether your response covers all the required points, and _Organization_ for readability and style. Like the journals, each category will be graded along a 4-point scale. **Unlike** the journals, this 4-point scale will be converted directly to point values: your total score will be multiplied by 2.5 (for a maximum possible 40 points).

As noted above, this part of the assignment is done in groups. The week before the midterm, a "dummy" assignment will be released on Gradescope titled "Midterm Group Formation". You must "submit" this assignment before the week of the midterm to let me know who you are working with (note: if you want, these groups can be the same groups you end up working with for the final project. But that is up to you!).

There is **no time limit** for this part of the exam. On **Monday, October 27**, I will directly contact your group via email to give you your assigned proposal (each group will get a different proposal, though the proposals will be re-used between sections). Then, you have the entire week to work on it on your own schedule. Your writeup is **due by Friday, October 31 at 5:00 pm** by submitting a PDF on Gradescope.

This part of the exam is **open everything**. You can use any resources you want with no limits. The only caveat is that the usual policies apply: external resources must be properly cited, and if you use generative AI, proper citation includes providing the full text of your prompt(s). **You can attach a references/bibliography section to your submission and this will not count towards the page limit**.

In particular, you are welcome (and encouraged!) to discuss with other groups in the class. Since each group has a different proposal, you might be able to get some fresh insights from someone who isn't actively working on your exact proposal! Again, if you choose to discuss with other groups, make sure you properly credit them.

## Before the midterm

We will host the following class activities to help you prepare for the midterm:

- We will create a shared Google Doc (combined across both sections) that you can use to create a **shared study guide**. In this doc, you can write down any definitions, proofs/derivations, etc. that you think will be useful. Everyone **must contribute at least one thing to the shared study guide**; there will be a Gradescope assignment where we ask you to submit a screenshot as proof you contributed something. **Contributing to the shared study guide is worth 10 points on the exam** (making up the "missing" 10 points from the case study). I'm not grading the quality of your contribution, so this is basically 10 free points on the exam&mdash;as long as you remember to do it!
- There will be an in-class midterm prep session on **Thursday, October 23**. Here, you can ask me any questions you might have, and I will also come to class with practice problems you can work on, to give you a sense of what kinds of questions to expect on the individual portion of the exam.

## During the midterm

There will be no regular class during the week of the midterm. However, I will be physically present in the classroom from 1:15pm to 4pm on Tuesday and Thursday (that's our combined class times for Sections 1 and 2). **If you want**, you can choose to use this as a time to work on the individual portion of the exam&mdash;with the benefit that I will be there to answer any clarifying questions you might have (clarifying questions only; I won't answer conceptual questions, but I can help if you think a question is ambiguously worded). Alternatively, you may choose to use this as a study period (though in that case I ask that you study quitely so as to not disturb anyone who is choosing to take the exam there). Finally, you can choose not to use this time block at all; it is completely optional and just there as an additional resource for you.

## After the midterm

When I grade your multiple-choice / short-answer questions, I will not provide full explanations of what you got wrong; I will only tell you whether or not you got the answer right, and if not, what the right answer was. **You can recover up to 50% of points lost on the individual portion of the exam** by following these instructions:
1. Book an appointment with Prof Chang any time before the end of the semester, to discuss the questions you got wrong.
2. Within 24 hours after the appointment, submit a written explanation, in your own words, of why your original answer was wrong and what the right answer was. For each question you submit an explanation for, you can recover up to 50% of lost points for that question (the exact number of points you get back is up to Prof Chang's discretion based on what exactly you say in your explanation, but the number will not be zero).
